Privacy policy

Crowd Finder, effective October 20, 2026

The short version

Crowd Finder does not collect, store or sell your data. I do not run any servers for it. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no trackers. The only time the app uses the internet is when you choose to download a map, as explained below.

What the app uses, and why

  • Location. To work out where you are, so your friends can see how far away you are and which way to walk. The app asks for location only while it is open.
  • Bluetooth and the local network. To connect directly to the phones of the people in your group.
  • Nearby Interaction (Ultra Wideband). To measure the exact distance and direction to a friend who is close to you.
  • Your name and group code. You type these. They tell your friends who you are and which group you are in.
  • Your screen. The app turns the brightness up and lights your screen in your group color when you or a friend asks for it.
  • Offline maps. When you choose to download a map of an event or hike, the app asks a public OpenStreetMap data service for the roads, paths, water and places in the area you picked, and saves them on your phone.
  • Meetup spots. A spot you drop on the map has a name and a position. It is shared with your group the same way your position is.
  • Group chat. The messages you type are shared with your group. Your name is shown next to them. If you report a message, you decide whether to send the email. It holds the message, the sender's name and an anonymous phone ID, and the app sends nothing by itself.
  • Meeting times and notifications. A meeting time you set has a title, a time and optionally a meetup spot. It is shared with your group. If you choose a reminder, the app asks permission to send notifications and schedules them on your phone.

Who can see it

While you are in a group, your name, your color and your position are sent directly from your phone to the phones of the people nearby who typed the same group code. This travels from phone to phone over Bluetooth and WiFi. It does not pass through any server, and I never receive it.

Anyone who types your group code can join your group and see this information. Share your group code only with people you trust.

If a friend in your group is too far away to link with directly, their name and position can reach you through other phones in the group that are in between. Each phone passes it along only to phones in the same group, over the same direct phone to phone link, and nothing passes through a server.

Meetup spots and meeting times you set are sent the same way, directly between the phones in your group, and anyone in your group can see them and remove them. Chat messages are sent the same way, so everyone in your group can read them. Which reminders you choose stays on your phone and is not shared.

What is stored

Stored on your phone only: your name, group code, color choice and settings, plus a random ID that lets your friends' phones recognize your phone when you rejoin. Your friends' positions are held in memory while the app is open and are not saved. Leaving the group or closing the app clears them. Nothing is sent to me or to anyone else.

Maps you download are saved on your phone until you delete them from the map menu or delete the app. Meetup spots are saved on your phone for the group they belong to and are cleared automatically after 36 hours. Meeting times are saved the same way and cleared about six hours after they start. Chat messages are saved on your phone for 24 hours. Reminders are scheduled with iOS on your phone and are deleted when you remove the meeting or leave the group.

Third parties

The app contains no advertising or analytics code. It uses Apple's own software for everything except the map download.

When you download a map, your phone sends the outline of the area you picked to a public OpenStreetMap data service, the Overpass API, which is run by the OpenStreetMap community and volunteers. Like any internet request, this shows the service your IP address. I do not receive any of it, and the app does not send your name, group code or position. If you use the search box to find a place before downloading, that search is handled by Apple Maps and covered by Apple's privacy policy. You can avoid all of this by never downloading a map, and the rest of the app works the same.

Map data is copyright OpenStreetMap contributors and available under the Open Database License. See openstreetmap.org/copyright.

Children

The app does not collect information from anyone, including children.

Your choices

You can turn off Location, Bluetooth or Local Network for the app in the iOS Settings app at any time, although the app needs them to find your friends. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your phone.
